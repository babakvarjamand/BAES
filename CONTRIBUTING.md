# Contributing to BAES

## Babak AI Engineering Standard

**Project Status:** Under Development

---

## 1. Purpose

Thank you for your interest in the **Babak AI Engineering Standard
(BAES)**.

BAES is being developed as a vendor-neutral, technology-neutral,
language-neutral, framework-neutral, and OS-neutral engineering
standard for structured Human–AI collaboration across domains.

This document explains how external participants may contribute to the
public development of BAES.

---

## 2. Contribution Philosophy

BAES welcomes constructive participation.

Useful contributions may include:

- Questions
- Clarifications
- Technical observations
- Research references
- Counterexamples
- Case-study proposals
- Critical analysis
- Identification of ambiguities
- Identification of inconsistencies
- Suggestions for improvement
- Evidence relevant to publicly released BAES material

Contributions should help improve the engineering quality of the
standard.

---

## 3. Contribution Does Not Equal Authority

Submitting a contribution does not grant authority over BAES.

In particular, a contribution does not automatically grant authority to:

- Change the project scope
- Change project intent
- Establish a BAES principle
- Establish a normative requirement
- Change governance
- Publish restricted material
- Approve a release
- Modify the publication boundary

Contributions are inputs to the engineering process.

They are not automatically decisions.

---

## 4. Human Engineering Authority

BAES development remains under explicit human engineering authority.

AI systems may be used to assist with:

- Research
- Analysis
- Drafting
- Evaluation
- Documentation
- Classification
- Technical exploration

However, AI-generated material does not automatically become an
accepted BAES position.

Human review and appropriate project governance remain necessary for
significant decisions.

---

## 5. Ways to Contribute

### 5.1 Questions

Questions about publicly available BAES material are welcome.

A good question may identify:

- Ambiguity
- Missing definitions
- Conflicting interpretations
- Unclear applicability
- Potential unintended consequences

---

### 5.2 Issues

Issues may be used to report:

- Errors
- Inconsistencies
- Ambiguities
- Documentation problems
- Broken references
- Potential design problems
- Research questions
- Suggestions for improvement

Issues should describe the problem clearly and provide enough context
for evaluation.

---

### 5.3 Research Contributions

Relevant research may be proposed when it contributes to questions
within the public BAES research direction.

Where possible, research contributions should identify:

- Research question
- Method
- Evidence
- Findings
- Limitations
- Relevant BAES concepts

A research contribution does not automatically become BAES evidence or
a normative requirement.

---

### 5.4 Case Study Contributions

External participants may propose case studies that can help evaluate
BAES.

A proposed case study should identify, where applicable:

- Domain
- Problem
- Human participants
- AI participant
- Intended outcome
- Authority model
- Scope
- Evaluation criteria

Case studies are intended to test BAES, including its weaknesses.

---

### 5.5 Critical Review

Critical review is explicitly welcome.

Contributors are encouraged to identify:

- Weak assumptions
- Missing constraints
- Contradictions
- Unnecessary complexity
- Non-generalizable concepts
- Insufficient evidence
- Practical difficulties
- Potential failure modes

A contribution does not need to agree with BAES to be valuable.

---

## 6. Contribution Quality

Contributions should preferably be:

- Specific
- Evidence-aware
- Constructive
- Technically reasoned
- Clearly scoped
- Distinguishable between fact and interpretation

When making a factual claim, provide supporting evidence where
practical.

When presenting an interpretation or hypothesis, identify it as such.

---

## 7. Evidence

BAES distinguishes between:

```text
Claim
   ≠
Evidence
   ≠
Interpretation
   ≠
Decision
````

Contributors should avoid presenting assumptions or interpretations as
established facts.

Where evidence is uncertain, conflicting, incomplete, or preliminary,
that uncertainty should be stated explicitly.

---

## 8. Scope Control

Contributions should remain within the publicly declared scope of BAES.

A contributor should not assume that a proposed extension is
automatically part of the project.

Potential scope expansions should be explicitly identified as such.

The project does not accept autonomous scope expansion through
contributions, pull requests, AI-generated content, or other means.

---

## 9. Public and Restricted Material

The public repository does not contain every BAES development artifact.

Some material may remain restricted, including:

* Unreleased foundational research
* Internal conceptual development
* Preliminary research
* Internal analysis
* Unpublished ontology work
* Unreleased specification material
* Other material subject to publication controls

Contributors should not attempt to publish restricted material.

See:

`docs/publication-policy.md`

---

## 10. Pull Requests

Where pull requests are enabled for a particular part of the project,
contributors should:

1. Clearly describe the proposed change.
2. Explain why the change is necessary.
3. Identify affected documents.
4. Identify relevant evidence where applicable.
5. Distinguish corrections from proposed changes.
6. Identify potential consequences.
7. Avoid unrelated changes.

A pull request is a proposal for evaluation.

It is not automatically an accepted modification to BAES.

---

## 11. Changes to Normative Material

Changes to future normative BAES material may require additional
review.

A proposed normative change should eventually provide sufficient
information to determine:

* What requirement is changing.
* Why it is changing.
* What evidence supports the change.
* What other artifacts are affected.
* Whether applicability changes.
* Whether conformance changes.
* Whether a release record is required.

The exact approval mechanism will be defined by BAES governance as the
standard matures.

---

## 12. Changes to Governance

Changes to BAES governance should not be introduced indirectly through
ordinary documentation edits.

Governance changes should be explicitly identified and evaluated
according to the applicable governance process.

---

## 13. Changes to Foundation

Unreleased BAES Foundation material is not part of the ordinary public
contribution workflow.

External contributions should not attempt to:

* Reconstruct restricted Foundation material
* Publish internal Foundation material
* Replace unreleased Foundation decisions
* Represent private development material as public specification

Foundation publication will occur only through an explicit project
publication decision.

---

## 14. AI-Assisted Contributions

AI-assisted contributions are permitted where appropriate.

However, contributors remain responsible for the material they submit.

AI-generated content should not be treated as authoritative merely
because it was produced by an AI system.

Contributors should review AI-generated material for:

* Accuracy
* Unsupported claims
* Fabricated references
* Inconsistent terminology
* Scope expansion
* Hidden assumptions
* Incorrect interpretation

Where relevant, contributors should disclose significant AI assistance.

---

## 15. Review Process

Contributions may be:

* Accepted
* Accepted with modification
* Deferred
* Requested for clarification
* Rejected
* Redirected to research
* Redirected to a case study
* Kept outside the public repository

A rejected contribution is not necessarily considered incorrect.

It may simply be outside the current scope, maturity, or publication
boundary of BAES.

---

## 16. Attribution

Contributors whose work is incorporated into publicly released BAES
material should receive appropriate attribution where applicable.

The exact attribution mechanism may evolve as the project governance
and licensing model mature.

---

## 17. Licensing

The licensing model for BAES and its associated materials is currently
under consideration.

Until the applicable licensing terms are formally established,
contributors should not assume that submission grants unrestricted
rights to redistribute or relicense BAES material.

---

## 18. Code of Conduct

Contributions should remain professional and focused on the engineering
subject.

Disagreement is welcome.

Personal attacks, harassment, deliberate misinformation, and attempts
to manipulate the project process are not considered constructive
contributions.

---

## 19. Public Engineering Record

Accepted public contributions may become part of the public engineering
record of BAES.

Where a contribution materially affects the evolution of the standard,
the resulting decision or change should remain traceable.

The objective is to preserve not only the resulting state of BAES but,
where appropriate, the engineering reasoning behind its evolution.

---

## 20. What Makes a Strong Contribution?

A strong contribution typically answers:

```text
What is the problem?
        ↓
Why does it matter?
        ↓
What evidence supports the observation?
        ↓
What change is being proposed?
        ↓
What would the consequences be?
        ↓
How could the proposal be evaluated?
```

The stronger the traceability and reasoning, the easier the
contribution is to evaluate.

## 22. Final Principle

BAES welcomes participation while maintaining controlled engineering
authority.

The working principle is:

> **Contributions may influence BAES; contributions do not
> automatically govern BAES.**
