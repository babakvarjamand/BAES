# BAES Case Studies

**Document Status:** Public Case Study Framework  
**Project Status:** Under Development  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-20

---

## 1. Purpose

The `case-studies/` area defines the public framework for evaluating
BAES in real-world Human–AI collaboration contexts.

Case studies are intended to examine whether BAES concepts and
requirements remain meaningful when applied to actual problem-solving
activities.

The purpose of a case study is not merely to demonstrate BAES.

It is to **evaluate BAES**.

---

## 2. Why Case Studies Matter

A standard may appear coherent at the conceptual level while producing
unexpected difficulties when applied in practice.

Case studies provide an opportunity to investigate questions such as:

- Can BAES be applied consistently?
- Are its concepts understandable?
- Are authority boundaries operationally meaningful?
- Can delegated authority be identified?
- Can scope be established and maintained?
- Can significant actions be traced?
- Can evidence be evaluated?
- Can human approval points be identified?
- Can the resulting collaboration be reviewed?
- Does BAES remain useful across different domains?

Case studies therefore form part of the validation process rather than
serving only as examples or demonstrations.

---

## 3. Case Study Philosophy

BAES case studies should seek to evaluate the standard rather than
simply confirm its assumptions.

A useful case study should therefore be capable of revealing:

- Ambiguity
- Missing requirements
- Inconsistent terminology
- Unnecessary complexity
- Scope failures
- Authority conflicts
- Traceability gaps
- Evidence limitations
- Domain-specific constraints
- Unexpected outcomes

A case study that exposes a weakness in BAES is potentially as valuable
as one that demonstrates successful application.

---

## 4. Domain Generality

BAES is intended to operate across domains.

Case studies may therefore eventually cover areas such as:

- Software engineering
- Scientific research
- Education
- Technical engineering
- Knowledge work
- Research assistance
- Analysis
- Decision support
- Design
- Other structured Human–AI collaboration contexts

The selection of a domain does not imply that BAES is primarily a
standard for that domain.

Instead, different domains may be used to evaluate whether the
underlying BAES concepts remain applicable beyond a single context.

---

## 5. Case Study Categories

Future case studies may be organized according to several dimensions.

### 5.1 Domain

The professional or technical environment in which the collaboration
takes place.

### 5.2 Problem Type

The nature of the problem being addressed.

Examples may include:

- Analytical
- Creative
- Technical
- Research-oriented
- Decision-support
- Planning
- Implementation

### 5.3 AI Participation

The role performed by the AI system.

Examples may include:

- Assistant
- Analyst
- Research participant
- Generator
- Evaluator
- Planner
- Executor

### 5.4 Authority Model

The type and extent of authority delegated to the AI participant.

### 5.5 Risk and Consequence

The potential impact of an incorrect, unauthorized, or irreversible
action.

These categories are intended as analytical dimensions rather than
mandatory classifications for every case study.

---

## 6. Case Study Structure

A mature BAES case study is expected to contain, where applicable:

```text
1. Case Study Identification
2. Problem Context
3. Intended Outcome
4. Human Participants
5. AI Participant(s)
6. Initial Scope
7. Authority Model
8. Delegated Authority
9. Constraints
10. Collaboration Process
11. Actions and Decisions
12. Evidence
13. Human Approvals
14. Deviations and Exceptions
15. Outcome
16. Evaluation
17. Findings
18. Limitations
19. BAES Implications
````

The final schema will be defined as the case-study methodology matures.

---

## 7. Initial Conditions

A case study should establish its initial conditions clearly enough
that the collaboration can be understood and evaluated.

Where applicable, this should include:

* Problem definition
* Human intent
* Intended outcome
* Initial scope
* Known constraints
* Participants
* AI capabilities
* Authority boundaries
* Evaluation criteria

The purpose is to distinguish the initial state from changes that
occur during the collaboration.

---

## 8. Human Intent

Case studies should explicitly identify the intended objective of the
human participant or governing authority.

This is important because BAES distinguishes between:

```text
Human Intent
     ↓
AI Interpretation
     ↓
AI Action
```

A case study should therefore make it possible to determine whether an
observed AI action remained consistent with the authorized intent.

---

## 9. Authority and Delegation

Where AI participation includes delegated actions, the case study
should identify:

* What authority was delegated.
* To whom or to what it was delegated.
* For what purpose.
* Within what boundaries.
* Under what conditions.
* Whether approval was required.
* Whether the delegation could be revoked.

Delegated authority should not automatically be interpreted as general
authority over the entire case.

---

## 10. Scope

Case studies should identify the authorized scope of the AI
participant.

Where relevant, the study should record:

* Initial scope
* Scope changes
* Requested expansions
* Authorized expansions
* Unauthorized expansions
* Scope-related inquiries
* Scope violations

This allows the case study to evaluate whether BAES mechanisms for
scope control are practical.

---

## 11. Engineering Inquiry

Case studies may include situations in which the AI participant
encounters:

* Ambiguity
* Missing information
* Conflicting instructions
* Insufficient authority
* Uncertain evidence
* Unclear scope
* Unexpected conditions

Where applicable, the study should record how such situations were
handled.

This may provide evidence for evaluating the BAES concept of
**Engineering Inquiry**.

---

## 12. Traceability

A case study should preserve sufficient information to reconstruct
important parts of the collaboration.

Depending on the case, this may include:

* Human instructions
* AI responses
* Decisions
* Approvals
* Actions
* Scope changes
* Evidence
* Exceptions
* Final outcomes

The level of required traceability may depend on the risk and nature
of the case.

---

## 13. Evidence

Case studies should distinguish between:

* AI-generated content
* Human observations
* External evidence
* Derived conclusions
* Decisions
* Final outcomes

An AI-generated statement should not automatically be classified as
independent evidence merely because it was produced by an AI system.

---

## 14. Evaluation

Case studies should evaluate both the **process** and the **outcome**
where appropriate.

Possible evaluation dimensions include:

* Correctness
* Completeness
* Relevance
* Scope adherence
* Authority adherence
* Evidence quality
* Traceability
* Human approval
* Efficiency
* Reliability
* Outcome quality

Not every dimension will be applicable to every case.

Evaluation criteria should be explicitly stated rather than assumed.

---

## 15. Failure and Deviation Reporting

Case studies should record significant failures and deviations.

Examples include:

* Incorrect AI output
* Unsupported claims
* Scope expansion
* Authority confusion
* Missing approval
* Traceability failure
* Evidence failure
* Human misunderstanding
* Unexpected interaction
* Process breakdown

Failures should not be removed merely because they produce an
unfavorable impression of BAES.

A failure may reveal an important limitation or improvement opportunity.

---

## 16. Negative Findings

BAES case studies should preserve significant negative findings.

A case study may conclude that:

* A BAES mechanism was ineffective.
* A requirement was ambiguous.
* A concept was unnecessary.
* A control created excessive overhead.
* A domain required additional mechanisms.
* A proposed mechanism did not generalize.
* A particular implementation introduced unexpected problems.

Such findings are part of the engineering evidence of the standard.

---

## 17. Case Study and Validation

Case studies contribute to validation but do not automatically prove
that BAES is correct.

Conceptually:

```text
Case Study
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Finding
    ↓
Evaluation
    ↓
Evidence Assessment
    ↓
BAES Revision or Confirmation
```

The strength of the resulting evidence depends on the methodology,
quality of the case, limitations, and other available evidence.

---

## 18. Cross-Domain Comparison

When multiple case studies exist, they may eventually be compared
across domains.

Such comparison may investigate:

* Common Human–AI structures
* Domain-specific differences
* Authority patterns
* Scope patterns
* Evidence requirements
* Review mechanisms
* Failure modes
* BAES applicability boundaries

Cross-domain comparison may provide evidence regarding the
domain-generality of BAES.

---

## 19. Case Study Status

Case studies may use statuses such as:

* Proposed
* Planned
* In Progress
* Completed
* Under Review
* Published
* Superseded

The final lifecycle and transition rules remain under development.

---

## 20. Publication Boundary

Not every case study is automatically public.

Internal case studies may remain unpublished when they contain:

* Unreleased BAES mechanisms
* Foundational research
* Preliminary results
* Internal evaluation material
* Sensitive information
* Work that has not reached publication maturity

Public case studies should be released only after an explicit
publication decision.

See:

`docs/publication-policy.md`

---

## 21. Case Study Traceability

Where practical, published case studies should be traceable to the
BAES concepts, principles, or requirements they evaluate.

A future relationship may resemble:

```text
BAES Concept
      ↓
Case Study
      ↓
Observation
      ↓
Finding
      ↓
Evaluation
      ↓
Evidence
      ↓
Specification Revision
```

This relationship is intended to make practical validation
reconstructable.

---

## 22. Case Studies Must Not Become Marketing Material

BAES case studies are engineering artifacts.

They should not be designed merely to demonstrate that BAES works.

A credible case-study program should allow:

* Successful outcomes
* Partial successes
* Failures
* Contradictory results
* Limitations
* Unresolved questions

to be recorded honestly.

---

## 23. Current Case Study Position

The BAES case-study framework is currently being established.

No claim is made at this stage that a complete cross-domain validation
program has been performed.

The immediate objective is to define a rigorous structure for future
case studies before publicly presenting conclusions based on them.

---

## 24. Guiding Principle

The current working principle is:

> **A case study should test the standard, not merely demonstrate it.**
