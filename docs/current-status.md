# BAES — Current Status

**Document Status:** Working Record
**Project Status:** Under Development
**Standard Status:** Not Yet Released
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-19

---

## 1. Purpose of This Record

This document records the current development state of the **Babak AI Engineering Standard (BAES)**.

It is intended to provide a transparent snapshot of what has been established, what is under development, what remains unresolved, and what requires further research or validation.

This document is a **status record**, not the BAES specification itself.

---

## 2. What BAES Is

**BAES (Babak AI Engineering Standard)** is a vendor-neutral standard for systematically designing, governing, and evaluating **Human–AI collaboration for real-world problem solving across domains**.

BAES is not limited to software engineering.

Software engineering is one potential application domain of BAES, but the standard is intended to address Human–AI collaboration wherever humans and AI systems participate together in structured problem-solving activities.

BAES is intended to remain:

* Vendor-neutral
* Technology-neutral
* Language-neutral
* Framework-neutral
* Operating-system-neutral
* Domain-general

---

## 3. Current Development State

BAES is currently in an active foundational development stage.

The project has progressed beyond an initial conceptual idea and has established a growing body of documented foundations, principles, discovery records, and governance concepts.

However, BAES is **not yet a finalized or formally released standard**.

The current work is focused on establishing a rigorous conceptual, ontological, governance, and evidentiary foundation before defining a mature normative specification.

---

## 4. Established Development Areas

The following areas have been actively investigated and documented during the development of BAES:

### 4.1 Foundation

The BAES foundation has been established around the principle that Human–AI collaboration should be treated as an engineering activity rather than as an informal interaction.

The foundation emphasizes, among other concerns:

* Documentation First
* Approval Before Implementation
* Modular Documentation
* Independent Reviewability
* Versioned Evolution
* Extensible Architecture
* Explicit Human Engineering Authority

---

### 4.2 Human–AI Authority

BAES distinguishes between human responsibility and AI participation.

A central concept under development is:

> **Authority may be delegated; engineering responsibility is not transferred.**

This work includes investigation of:

* Human Engineering Authority
* Delegated Authority
* Governance Authority
* Execution Authority
* Scope Authority
* Authority boundaries
* Controlled delegation

---

### 4.3 Scope Control

BAES recognizes the prevention of unauthorized scope expansion as a fundamental engineering concern.

A major principle under development is:

> **AI must not autonomously expand human-defined intent, scope, or objectives.**

The implications and formal requirements of this principle remain subject to continued specification and validation.

---

### 4.4 Traceability

Traceability is being developed as a core property of BAES.

The intended direction is that significant Human–AI engineering decisions, actions, changes, and outcomes should be sufficiently traceable to support review and reconstruction.

The exact normative requirements for traceability remain under development.

---

### 4.5 Engineering Inquiry

BAES includes the concept of **Engineering Inquiry** as a mechanism for identifying uncertainty, ambiguity, missing information, conflicts, or conditions requiring clarification before execution.

The role and formal treatment of Engineering Inquiry remain under development.

---

### 4.6 Ontology Discovery

BAES has entered an explicit ontology-discovery process.

The purpose of this work is to identify and define the fundamental entities, relationships, states, authorities, evidence types, actions, decisions, and other constructs required for a coherent Human–AI engineering model.

Ontology discovery is not considered complete.

---

### 4.7 MOU / Datum / Coordinate Discovery

The project has investigated the relationship between **MOU, Datum, and Coordinate** concepts as part of establishing a rigorous reference framework for Human–AI collaboration.

This work remains part of the foundational conceptual development and is not yet treated as a finalized normative model.

---

## 5. Status and Evidence

BAES explicitly distinguishes between the **status of a concept or decision** and the **validity or strength of the evidence supporting it**.

A concept may therefore be:

* Identified
* Proposed
* Candidate
* Established
* Deprecated
* Rejected
* Unresolved

while its supporting evidence may independently vary in validity, completeness, reliability, or maturity.

Accordingly:

> **Concept Status and Evidence Validity are distinct dimensions.**

This distinction is considered important for preventing premature conversion of hypotheses, observations, or candidate principles into normative requirements.

---

## 6. Current Standard Maturity

BAES should currently be considered a **developmental engineering standard project**, not a completed standard.

At the current stage:

* The conceptual foundation is under active development.
* Core principles have been identified and are being refined.
* The ontology is under discovery.
* Governance concepts are under development.
* Evidence requirements are being clarified.
* Validation methodology has not yet been fully finalized.
* The normative specification is not yet complete.
* External review has not yet been completed.
* No formal conformance or certification regime has yet been established.

---

## 7. What Is Not Yet Finalized

The following areas require additional work before BAES can be considered a mature public specification:

1. Complete scope and terminology.
2. Formal ontology.
3. Normative requirements.
4. Conformance model.
5. Evidence model.
6. Governance model.
7. Change-management model.
8. Validation methodology.
9. Independent review mechanisms.
10. Case-study methodology.
11. Release criteria.
12. Long-term maintenance and evolution model.

These items should not be interpreted as failures or deficiencies. They represent the remaining engineering work required to move from a developing standard to a mature and reviewable specification.

---

## 8. Current Project Principle

The BAES project itself is intended to follow the engineering principles it seeks to define.

Therefore, BAES development should prioritize:

**Documentation before implementation.**

**Explicit decisions before irreversible changes.**

**Traceability before assumption.**

**Evidence before normative claims.**

**Human authority over project intent and governance.**

**Controlled evolution over uncontrolled scope expansion.**

---

## 9. Current Repository Role

The BAES GitHub repository is intended to function as the:

> **Public Engineering Record of the BAES project.**

It is therefore not merely a document repository or promotional project page.

The repository is intended to progressively record:

* Definitions
* Principles
* Specifications
* Research
* Evidence
* Case studies
* Decisions
* Reviews
* Changes
* Releases
* Governance
* Project evolution

The repository structure and documentation model will evolve as BAES itself matures.

## 10. Next Development Priorities

The immediate priorities are:

1. Establish the BAES repository architecture.
2. Record the current state of the project.
3. Define the development roadmap.
4. Consolidate existing foundational decisions and records.
5. Continue ontology discovery.
6. Formalize terminology and conceptual relationships.
7. Develop the governance model.
8. Define the evidence and validation model.
9. Establish the path toward a normative specification.
10. Define criteria for future public releases.

Public dissemination, professional outreach, and archival publication are intentionally deferred until the foundational documentation and development path are sufficiently mature.

## 12. Status Statement

**BAES is an actively developing, domain-general engineering standard for systematically designing, governing, and evaluating Human–AI collaboration for real-world problem solving.**

The project has established a foundational body of concepts and principles but has not yet reached a finalized normative specification or formal public release.

The current priority is disciplined foundation-building, documentation, ontology discovery, governance design, evidence development, and validation.
