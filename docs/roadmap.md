# BAES Development Roadmap

**Document Status:** Working Record
**Project Status:** Under Development
**Roadmap Version:** 0.1
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-19

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the development roadmap for the **Babak AI Engineering Standard (BAES)**.

The roadmap describes the intended path from the current foundational development state toward a mature, reviewable, and publicly released engineering standard for systematically designing, governing, and evaluating Human–AI collaboration for real-world problem solving across domains.

This roadmap is itself a living engineering record.

It may be revised as new evidence, discoveries, constraints, or requirements emerge.

Changes to the roadmap must remain traceable to the project's documented decisions and development records.

---

# 2. Development Philosophy

BAES development follows a deliberately staged process.

The project does not treat the existence of a concept, principle, or hypothesis as sufficient justification for making it a normative requirement.

The intended progression is:

```text
Problem / Need
      ↓
Inquiry
      ↓
Discovery
      ↓
Conceptualization
      ↓
Evidence
      ↓
Validation
      ↓
Formalization
      ↓
Review
      ↓
Release
      ↓
Evolution
```

This progression is intended to reduce premature standardization and preserve traceability between claims, decisions, evidence, and requirements.

---

# 3. Roadmap Principles

The development roadmap is governed by the following principles:

### 3.1 Documentation First

Significant project knowledge and decisions should be documented before implementation or formalization.

### 3.2 Evidence Before Normative Claims

A candidate principle should not automatically become a normative requirement merely because it appears reasonable.

### 3.3 Explicit Authority

Human authority over project intent, scope, and governance must remain explicit.

### 3.4 Controlled Scope Evolution

BAES must not expand its scope implicitly.

Changes to scope should be identified, evaluated, documented, and approved.

### 3.5 Independent Reviewability

The project should progressively develop artifacts and records that allow significant claims and decisions to be independently examined.

### 3.6 Traceable Evolution

Changes to BAES should remain reconstructable through documented records.

### 3.7 Minimal Foundation

The standard should establish only the foundational constructs necessary to support its intended purpose.

Additional constructs should be introduced only when justified by identified requirements, evidence, or unresolved engineering needs.

---

# 4. Development Phases

The current roadmap consists of the following major phases:

```text
Phase 0 — Foundation and Scope
        ↓
Phase 1 — Ontology and Core Concepts
        ↓
Phase 2 — Formal Specification
        ↓
Phase 3 — Research and Evidence
        ↓
Phase 4 — Case Studies and Validation
        ↓
Phase 5 — Public Review
        ↓
Phase 6 — First Formal Release
        ↓
Phase 7 — Post-Release Evolution
```

The phases are sequential in intent but may overlap where necessary.

A later phase must not be interpreted as automatically validating the outputs of an earlier phase.

---

# 5. Phase 0 — Foundation and Scope

## Objective

Establish the foundational identity, purpose, scope, and engineering principles of BAES.

## Key Questions

* What is BAES?
* What problem does BAES address?
* What is within the scope of BAES?
* What is outside its scope?
* What does Human–AI collaboration mean in the context of BAES?
* What authority belongs to humans?
* What role may AI systems perform?
* What foundational constraints must exist?

## Major Work

* Establish BAES purpose.
* Establish domain-general scope.
* Identify foundational principles.
* Establish Human Engineering Authority.
* Establish initial authority boundaries.
* Establish the principle of controlled delegation.
* Establish the principle of no autonomous scope expansion.
* Establish documentation-first development.
* Establish initial traceability requirements.
* Record foundational decisions.
* Identify unresolved foundational questions.

## Current State

This phase has produced substantial foundational material and is transitioning into continued conceptual and ontological development.

## Exit Criteria

Phase 0 may be considered complete when:

* BAES purpose is sufficiently defined.
* Scope boundaries are explicitly documented.
* Foundational principles are recorded.
* Fundamental unresolved questions are identified.
* Foundation decisions are traceable.
* The relationship between human authority and AI participation is sufficiently articulated for ontology development.

---

# 6. Phase 1 — Ontology and Core Concepts

## Objective

Develop a coherent conceptual model of the entities, relationships, states, authorities, actions, decisions, evidence, and interactions required by BAES.

## Key Questions

* What are the fundamental entities in Human–AI engineering?
* What constitutes an engineering action?
* What constitutes a decision?
* What constitutes intent?
* What constitutes scope?
* What forms of authority exist?
* What is delegated authority?
* What is evidence?
* What is a status?
* What is validity?
* How are these concepts related?
* Which concepts are foundational and which are derived?

## Major Work

* Continue ontology discovery.
* Define core terminology.
* Identify relationships between concepts.
* Resolve ambiguity between similar constructs.
* Define state models where necessary.
* Define authority relationships.
* Define evidence-related concepts.
* Investigate MOU / Datum / Coordinate relationships.
* Distinguish candidate concepts from established concepts.
* Distinguish conceptual status from evidence validity.
* Record unresolved ontology questions.

## Exit Criteria

Phase 1 may be considered complete when:

* Core concepts have explicit definitions.
* Core relationships have been documented.
* Major terminology ambiguities have been resolved or explicitly recorded.
* Foundational ontology decisions are traceable.
* Remaining ontology gaps are understood.
* The conceptual model is sufficiently stable to support normative specification.

---

# 7. Phase 2 — Formal Specification

## Objective

Translate the sufficiently mature conceptual model into a structured normative specification.

## Key Questions

* What does BAES require?
* What does BAES permit?
* What does BAES prohibit?
* Which requirements are normative?
* What constitutes compliance?
* What evidence is required to demonstrate compliance?
* Which requirements apply universally?
* Which requirements are conditional or context-dependent?

## Major Work

* Define specification architecture.
* Define normative terminology.
* Formalize requirements.
* Establish requirement identifiers.
* Establish requirement traceability.
* Define applicability conditions.
* Define conformance concepts.
* Map requirements to principles.
* Map requirements to evidence.
* Identify unresolved normative questions.

## Exit Criteria

Phase 2 may be considered complete when:

* The normative structure is coherent.
* Requirements are explicitly identifiable.
* Requirements are traceable to their conceptual basis.
* Applicability is defined.
* Conformance is sufficiently specified for evaluation.
* Major normative ambiguities are resolved or documented.

---

# 8. Phase 3 — Research and Evidence

## Objective

Develop and evaluate evidence supporting BAES claims, principles, models, and requirements.

## Key Questions

* Which BAES claims require empirical evidence?
* Which claims can be supported conceptually?
* Which claims require experimentation?
* How should Human–AI collaboration be evaluated?
* What constitutes valid evidence?
* How should evidence quality be assessed?
* How should contradictory evidence be handled?

## Major Work

* Define research questions.
* Define research methodologies.
* Conduct controlled experiments where appropriate.
* Evaluate AI-assisted problem solving.
* Study Human–AI authority models.
* Evaluate traceability mechanisms.
* Evaluate scope-control mechanisms.
* Record observations.
* Separate observation from interpretation.
* Assess evidence validity.
* Record limitations and uncertainty.

## Exit Criteria

Phase 3 may be considered complete when:

* Major evidence requirements are identified.
* Relevant research has been conducted or explicitly deferred.
* Evidence is traceably associated with the claims it supports.
* Evidence limitations are documented.
* Unsupported assumptions have been identified.

---

# 9. Phase 4 — Case Studies and Validation

## Objective

Evaluate whether BAES can be applied consistently to real-world Human–AI problem-solving contexts.

## Potential Domains

Case studies may include, but are not limited to:

* Software engineering
* Scientific research
* Education
* Knowledge work
* Analysis and decision support
* Technical problem solving
* Other domains involving structured Human–AI collaboration

The selection of domains should be driven by the validation objectives rather than by an assumption that BAES belongs to any single domain.

## Major Work

* Define case-study methodology.
* Select representative problem contexts.
* Apply BAES systematically.
* Record human decisions.
* Record AI participation.
* Record authority boundaries.
* Record evidence.
* Evaluate traceability.
* Identify failures and limitations.
* Compare expected and observed outcomes.
* Identify domain-specific adaptation requirements.

## Exit Criteria

Phase 4 may be considered complete when:

* BAES has been evaluated in sufficiently representative contexts.
* Applicability boundaries are documented.
* Major failure modes are understood.
* Important ambiguities have been identified.
* Findings have been incorporated into the specification or explicitly recorded as unresolved.

---

# 10. Phase 5 — Public Review

## Objective

Expose a sufficiently mature BAES specification and supporting evidence to structured external review.

## Major Work

* Prepare public review materials.
* Define review scope.
* Establish feedback mechanisms.
* Collect technical criticism.
* Record proposed changes.
* Evaluate objections.
* Distinguish substantive defects from preference-based feedback.
* Record review outcomes.
* Revise the specification where justified.

## Exit Criteria

Phase 5 may be considered complete when:

* Public review has been conducted according to a documented process.
* Significant feedback has been evaluated.
* Accepted changes are incorporated.
* Rejected changes are documented with rationale.
* Remaining known limitations are explicitly recorded.

---

# 11. Phase 6 — First Formal Release

## Objective

Publish the first formal BAES release that satisfies defined maturity and review criteria.

## Major Work

* Freeze the release candidate.
* Complete specification review.
* Complete terminology review.
* Complete traceability review.
* Verify documentation consistency.
* Verify release artifacts.
* Assign a formal version.
* Publish the release.
* Archive the release record.

## Release Criteria

A formal release should require, at minimum:

* Defined scope.
* Defined terminology.
* Coherent normative specification.
* Traceable requirements.
* Documented evidence basis where applicable.
* Defined conformance approach.
* Documented known limitations.
* Completed required review.
* Complete release record.

A version must not be considered formal merely because the documents are publicly available.

---

# 12. Phase 7 — Post-Release Evolution

## Objective

Maintain and evolve BAES while preserving compatibility, traceability, and governance.

## Major Work

* Collect implementation feedback.
* Monitor new evidence.
* Review emerging Human–AI practices.
* Evaluate change proposals.
* Maintain compatibility information.
* Deprecate obsolete concepts where necessary.
* Introduce new requirements when justified.
* Publish revisions.
* Maintain historical records.

## Evolution Principle

BAES should evolve through controlled, documented change rather than uncontrolled accumulation of concepts and requirements.

---

# 13. Cross-Phase Workstreams

Some activities extend across multiple phases.

These include:

### Governance

Authority, decision-making, change control, and review.

### Documentation

Maintenance of the public engineering record.

### Traceability

Linking requirements, principles, decisions, evidence, and changes.

### Evidence

Maintaining the distinction between claims and their supporting evidence.

### Terminology

Maintaining consistent definitions across all BAES artifacts.

### Quality Assurance

Checking documentation consistency, logical coherence, and specification integrity.

---

# 14. Current Priority

As of the current roadmap version, the immediate priority is:

> **Establishing a rigorous and traceable foundation for BAES before formalizing the complete normative specification.**

The current work therefore prioritizes:

1. Repository architecture.
2. Foundation consolidation.
3. Ontology discovery.
4. Terminology.
5. Authority and governance concepts.
6. Evidence modeling.
7. Roadmap refinement.
8. Identification of unresolved questions.

Public dissemination is intentionally secondary to foundational development at this stage.

---

# 15. Future Public Dissemination

Public dissemination is planned only after the project reaches an appropriate level of maturity.

The intended future sequence is:

```text
Internal Development
        ↓
Public Engineering Record
        ↓
Mature Documentation
        ↓
Research / Evidence
        ↓
Public Review
        ↓
Formal Release
        ↓
Professional Dissemination
        ↓
Archival Publication / DOI
```

Professional dissemination channels such as LinkedIn are therefore treated as a later communication activity rather than as a substitute for engineering validation.

Archival publication mechanisms such as Zenodo may be considered for formal releases when the project reaches an appropriate maturity level.

---

# 16. Roadmap Governance

This roadmap is not a fixed promise.

It is a controlled project artifact.

Changes may be required when:

* New evidence changes an assumption.
* A foundational concept is revised.
* An ontology discovery changes the project structure.
* Validation reveals an unexpected limitation.
* External review identifies a significant issue.
* The scope of BAES is formally changed.

Significant roadmap changes should be documented and traceable.

---

# 17. Success Condition

The ultimate objective of the roadmap is not merely to produce a collection of documents.

The objective is to develop a standard that can be:

* Understood
* Applied
* Evaluated
* Reviewed
* Challenged
* Revised
* Cited
* Governed
* And independently examined

without requiring access to the private history of the project's development.

That requirement is central to the role of the BAES repository as the:

> **Public Engineering Record of BAES.**
